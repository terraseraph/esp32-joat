Joat Hardware: redesign directions

Three static mockups of the same product: a hardware manager that takes a board from idea to delivered boards. Each contains the designs home, the design workspace (schematic, PCB, 3D, code, checks, BOM, cost, numbered review doubts), the AI builder with its tool-call trail and accept/reject proposals, Settings for AI providers, the six-step order pipeline with an Orders list, and the parts library. Content is illustrative (prices, stock and some LCSC numbers are invented).

A · BENCH

Dense IDE workbench

Keeps the current tool's DNA and pushes it further: explorer, tabbed canvas, a bottom panel that shows the doubts list by default, and the AI builder docked on the right like an IDE assistant. Fastest for people who live in the editor; ordering is a separate full-page wizard with a vertical stepper and a running total.

Open A → B · PIPELINE

Stage-centric pipeline

Every board is a position on one pipeline: design, pre-flight, BOM, quote, shipping, review, tracking. The home is a board-by-stage matrix, a chevron stage bar stays on top of every board screen, and the workbench shows a "gate" of what blocks the next stage. Makes the manufacturing path and its blockers impossible to miss.

Open B → C · OVERVIEW

Calm dashboard-first

Each board opens on an overview with a plain-language readiness checklist, cost, parts, doubts and activity; the workspace is a focused canvas with a doubts/checks/BOM drawer and a collapsible AI dock. Roomier and least intimidating, best for people who design occasionally, at the cost of density for power users.
